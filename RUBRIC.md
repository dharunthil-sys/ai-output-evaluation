# Rating rubric

Each output is scored 1–5 on three dimensions, then one output is chosen as better overall. Every choice needs a written explanation that points to something specific: a region of the image, a timestamp, a word or a sound.

## Dimensions

| Score | Adherence (does it do what the prompt asked?) | Quality (is it well made?) | Realism (could this exist / sound like a person?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Every element of the prompt is present and correct | No visible or audible defects | Indistinguishable from real |
| 4 | One minor detail missing or slightly off | Small defect only visible on zoom or replay | One small giveaway |
| 3 | One clear element missing or wrong | Defect visible at normal size | Noticeably artificial in places |
| 2 | Several elements missing or wrong | Several defects or one severe one | Artificial throughout |
| 1 | Ignores the prompt | Broken or unusable | Not believable at all |

## What to check

- **Images:** zoom into hands, faces, text and edges. Count fingers. Read every word of any text in the image letter by letter.
- **Video:** watch once at normal speed, then replay and pause. Check for flicker, objects changing shape between frames, physics that looks wrong (a ball speeding up after a bounce), and lips or sound out of sync.
- **Text and voice:** check facts, whether the length and tone match the prompt, and whether it sounds like natural speech.

## Choosing when both outputs have flaws

Decide which flaw matters more for this prompt. Missing a prompt element usually outweighs a small visual defect, because the user asked for that element. State the deciding flaw in the explanation.

## Writing the explanation

- Say what you saw or heard, and where: "the left hand has six fingers", "the sign reads 'Leatherhaed'", "the ball changes size at 0:03".
- Give the reason for the choice in one sentence.
- No vague words like "better quality" without the detail behind them.
